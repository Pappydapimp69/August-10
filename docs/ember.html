Ember

Click anywhere to start a fire. The trees burn, the creatures run, and then — always — the world comes back. Not because fire is gentle, but because every fire carries a finite, dwindling budget of spreads. It cannot rage forever, by construction.

Everything here is a recorded lesson wearing a costume. The fire carries a generation budget because a radius-capped fire was once observed relighting the same cells forever — a burnt cell has no memory of having burned (the-answering-deep#E11). The trees are spaced by min-distance rejection because independent uniform scatter clumps — "looks random" and "looks even" are different properties (sandbox-scatter#E1). The creatures switch moods only when a new urge beats the current one by a real margin, because without hysteresis they thrash at every boundary. And the whole world grows from one seed with no ambient entropy, so world 7 is the same forest on every machine, forever. Night falls; they sleep; the burnt places sprout again.
